Four OpenAlex title-and-abstract searches covered wall functions for the Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) equations, LES wall conditions, inner--outer models and learned stress estimators (1 January 2000 to 16 November 2026). The accompanying protocol gives the strings, filters and deduplication. Of 2,543 screened records, 735 went forward, 171 had a candidate public retrieval route and 120 texts were obtained. The 115 reports reassessed here comprise 112 passing the full-text language check and three recorded language exclusions. Indexing and access limit coverage.

For a hydrodynamic comparison we require a named wall treatment or stress estimator, an identifiable test and a measured quantity with a comparator. Our primary scope is turbulent flow of a Newtonian fluid over smooth walls under incompressible or low-Mach conditions. In each report we distinguish flow, forcing, model and solver, then record observable, reference and use in calibration. Editions of one work count once. Runs reusing a flow, treatment or benchmark remain linked. We distinguish a restriction on admissible stress, a response to prescribed inputs, and a coupled LES or RANS result. A benchmark of either complete solver isolates a wall-treatment change only with matched controls. A heat-flux comparison has its own physical scope. An LES resolving the viscous sublayer with no wall treatment is a flow comparison only.

For comparisons of relative accuracy, we identify both calculations, then evaluate the change in discrepancy against a common reference within each quantity on stated spatial support. We traced 79 earlier pairings to their run and figure identities. Those 79 were selected for heterogeneous results and cannot supply the frequency of reversals. Physical uncertainty is kept distinct from rounding and graphical reading precision. A second automated reading of 34 pairings, given the first reading's quantities and locations, agreed on 31 of 34 same-run judgements and 12 of 24 finer categories.

The counts in Sec.~\ref{sec:evidence} rest on facts recorded under rules fixed in advance, with every label derived by script. All readings were automated. The first, of all 115 reports, saw the earlier record. A second, given only rules and sources, covered a random 30 of the 79 reports not used to write the rules and every candidate for the two rarer groups. A third, which saw both, settled each disagreement against the source. After settlement 108 reports are eligible, two contested and five excluded. On the random sample, agreement on a matched wall-treatment contrast was Cohen's $\kappa=0.87$ (lower 95\,\% bound 0.67). Both disagreements concerned near-wall methods on grids that reach the viscous sublayer. Clarifications written after that test, before the third reading, keep such methods in scope and mark them. Eleven eligible reports are boundary cases: five use those methods, three test stress estimators never presented as wall models and three had disputed eligibility. Counts are given with and without them. For the constitutive and transfer groups $\kappa$ was 0.67 and 0.86 (lower bounds 0.44 and 0.61). A count restricted to a change of model failed our stability criterion, and labels for complete-system benchmarks did not reproduce ($\kappa=0.15$). We quote neither.
