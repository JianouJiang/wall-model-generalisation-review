Four OpenAlex title-and-abstract searches covered wall functions for the Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) equations, LES wall conditions, inner--outer models and learned stress estimators (1 January 2000 to 16 November 2026). The accompanying protocol gives the strings, filters and deduplication. Of 2,543 screened records, 735 went forward, 171 had a candidate public retrieval route and 120 texts were obtained. The 115 reports reassessed here comprise 112 passing the full-text language check and three recorded language exclusions. Indexing and access limit coverage.

For a hydrodynamic comparison we require a named wall treatment or stress estimator, an identifiable test and a measured quantity with a comparator. Our primary scope is turbulent flow of a single-phase Newtonian fluid with constant properties and no buoyancy over smooth walls at Mach numbers up to 0.3. Of the 615 recorded tests, 159 state neither a Mach number nor compressibility and count as inside it. In each report we distinguish flow, forcing, model and solver, then record observable, reference and use in calibration. Editions of one work count once. Runs reusing a flow, treatment or benchmark remain linked. We distinguish a restriction on admissible stress, a response to prescribed inputs, and a coupled LES or RANS result. A benchmark of either complete solver isolates a wall-treatment change only with matched controls. A heat-flux comparison has its own physical scope. An LES resolving the viscous sublayer with no wall treatment is a flow comparison only.

For each matched contrast we recorded which treatment lies closer to the reference in every quantity that the source compares for both. A printed value decides a direction only if it holds for every value that rounds to the printed digits and against every reference given. A plotted curve decides one if the treatments differ by more than the stroke width over more than half of its range. Rule, sample and thresholds were fixed before any source was read. One automated reading covered all 136 contrasts in the archived public copy of each report, an arXiv version for 37 of the 58. A second, given only rule and sources, covered a random 20 of the 58 reports. The two gave the same category for 33 of 34 contrasts ($\kappa=0.94$, lower bound 0.76) and the same direction in 108 of 122 comparisons by pair and class of quantity ($\kappa=0.84$, lower bound 0.75). A third reading settled the six contrasts on which they differed and changed the category of two. The rule does not say whether a drawn zero line marks a separation point, or which stroke counts when the reference is drawn thicker. The factor separating two conditions was coded once. An earlier set of 79 pairings, selected for heterogeneous results, is not used.

The counts in Sec.~\ref{sec:evidence} rest on facts recorded under rules fixed in advance, with every label derived by script. All readings were automated. The first, of all 115 reports, saw the earlier record. A second, given only rules and sources, covered a random 30 of the 79 reports not used to write the rules and every candidate for the two rarer groups. A third, which saw both, settled each disagreement against the source. After settlement 108 reports are eligible, two contested and five excluded. The first two readings agreed on eligibility for 27 of the 30 sampled reports and 64 of the 74 read twice. On the random sample, agreement on a matched wall-treatment contrast was Cohen's $\kappa=0.87$ (lower 95\,\% bound 0.67). Both disagreements concerned near-wall methods on grids that reach the viscous sublayer. Clarifications written after that test, before the third reading, keep such methods in scope and mark them. Twelve eligible reports are boundary cases: five use those methods, four test stress estimators never presented as wall models and three had disputed eligibility. A fourth reading, done once, completed the record of first-cell spacing for every coupled design and recorded the flow regime of every counted test. It added the fourth estimator; eight reports state no first-cell distance in wall units and four test only flows outside the primary scope. A separate reading of the 34 contrasts read twice agreed with it on primary scope for all 34 and on first-cell class for 31. Counts are given for the collection, without boundary cases or contrasts on grids that reach the sublayer, and within the primary scope. For the constitutive and transfer groups $\kappa$ was 0.67 and 0.86 (lower bounds 0.44 and 0.61). The readings found 20 and 15 constitutive members; both found the 13 that are not boundary cases. A count restricted to a change of model failed our stability criterion, and labels for complete-system benchmarks did not reproduce ($\kappa=0.15$). We quote neither.
