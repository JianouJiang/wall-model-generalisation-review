# Current research status

The current manuscript is a corrected critical review prepared for expert feedback. It presents source-checked examples, a physical framework and a proposed checklist. It does not present the historical corpus as a settled quantitative analysis.

Completed corrections are documented in [scientific-corrections.md](scientific-corrections.md): source/version reconciliation for retained cases, explicit physical-scope uncertainty, separation of relative and absolute error, withdrawal of unsupported aggregates, and a limited interpretation of retrospective checklist records.

The source reassessment traces every retrieved work. For the standard periodic hill at bulk Reynolds number 10,595, five studies tabulate separation and reattachment against a reference for both crossings. A direction of change is called resolved when it holds for every value that rounds to the printed digits and against every reference in the table. Of the nine fixed-mesh wall-treatment comparisons, five move both crossings closer, two move them in opposite directions to the printed digits, one is unresolved (a one-decimal baseline and a reference-dependent reattachment change) and one has no baseline bubble. Of the two opposite cases, one source does not report its averaging interval and the other averaged its two runs over about 15 and 19 flow-through times. Neither reports a sampling uncertainty for a crossing. The data file keeps the second in a class of its own (`controls differ`) to record the different intervals. Ranking the point values alone gives five, three and one. In each of the three nominally opposite cases the smaller change is at most 0.05 hill heights. The studies reuse meshes, measurements and trained treatments. These tables do not establish a trade-off between the two crossings. Three group counts are quoted in the paper and rest on recorded facts. Rules fixed in advance say which facts to record for a wall-treatment contrast, a constitutive test and a transfer test, and a script derives every membership. A first automated reading applied the rules to all 115 works with the earlier record in view. A second, given only the rules and the sources, read a seeded random 30 of the 79 works that had not been read twice before, and every candidate for the constitutive and transfer groups (74 works in all). A third reading, which saw both, settled each disagreement against the source with quotations. After settlement 108 works are eligible, two are contested and five are excluded. The first two readings gave the same disposition for 27 of the 30 sampled works and for 64 of the 74 read twice. A fourth reading then recorded the first-cell spacing of the coupled designs and the flow regime of every counted test (below). Each count is given for the collection, without boundary cases and within the paper's primary scope.

- **Matched wall-treatment contrast.** 58 of 108 eligible works contain two coupled calculations that differ only in the wall treatment or its implementation and are compared with one quantitative reference for a hydrodynamic quantity; 61 if an unreported control is allowed. Without boundary cases the counts are 54 and 56 of 96; within the primary scope, 54 and 56 of 104. On the random sample Cohen's κ is 0.87 (lower 95 % bound 0.67). The count restricted to a change of model (50) is **not quotable**: its member count on the random sample changed from 12 to 10, more than the limit fixed in advance.
- **Scope boundary.** Both disagreements on the random sample concern near-wall methods on grids that reach the viscous sublayer, for which two rule texts gave different dispositions. Three clarifications were written after the agreement test and before the third reading. Twelve eligible works are marked as boundary cases: five sublayer-resolving methods, four stress estimators never presented as wall models (the fourth added by the fourth reading) and three with disputed eligibility.
- **Constitutive constraint.** 17 works (13 without boundary cases, 16 within the primary scope); κ 0.67 (lower bound 0.44) over the 44 candidate works. The two readings found 20 and 15 members and agreed on 14; both found all 13 that are not boundary cases.
- **Transfer.** 33 works test a fitted treatment at 110 conditions outside its fitting set. Nine tests in four works are qualified by a hold-out statement or by coefficients frozen by other authors (six in one study), 60 are partly exposed and 41 are unresolved. Without boundary cases: 102 conditions in 29 works (9, 55 and 38). Within the primary scope: 100 in 30 (8, 53 and 39); the qualified test that falls outside is a rough-wall channel, the only qualified test of its work. Whether a work has such a test: κ 0.86 (lower bound 0.61).
- **First-cell spacing and flow regime (fourth reading).** Under a plan and a regime rule fixed before it, one automated reading of all 108 eligible works recorded four regime facts (surface, compressibility with a threshold of Mach 0.3, thermal coupling, fluid) for 615 items: every design, every member or upper-bound contrast, every transfer test and every qualifying constitutive candidate. It also recorded the first-cell class of every coupled design in the 66 works that had no located class. All 530 quotations are found in the source text on or next to the stated page. Sixty items lie outside the primary scope. Four eligible works test a wall treatment only outside it (variable properties with buoyancy, Mach 6 to 7.7, a prescribed pipe roughness, a variable-property heat exchanger). A clarification written after the reading says that only a design testing a wall treatment or estimator can place a work inside the scope; it moves one work, and the value under the rule as first written (105) is stored beside the printed one (104). Among works with a wall treatment in a running solver, no stated design reaches the viscous sublayer in 81, both kinds occur in five, every design does in the five boundary works, and eight state no first-cell distance in wall units. A pattern search of the full texts was read as a cross-check: none of its 39 first-cell matches describes a member contrast on a grid stated to reach the sublayer, and no class was changed. ## Limits of the current work

- The collection is not exhaustive and its historical coding is not a certified census of current journal versions.
- Identified preprints are used as preprints; journal equivalence is not assumed.
- The framework is not an exhaustive or mutually exclusive causal taxonomy.
- Retrospective proposal agreement does not validate the checklist's predictive value.
- Absolute accuracy requires a relevant reference and uncertainty assessment; sensitivity alone cannot supply it.
- Resolved directions are resolved to printed digits. Sampling, discretisation and measurement uncertainties enter only where a source reports them.
- The third reading saw both earlier readings. It is not blind, and the first reading saw the earlier record. Agreement describes how reproducibly facts are assigned inside this collection, not whether the collection represents the literature.
- 41 works were read once under the fact rules. The 17 of them that hold a member contrast had every member contrast checked against the source by the third reading. A comparison made only by statement (clarification S3) could be found only in the 74 works read twice.
- First-cell spacing and flow regime were read once, by a reading that saw the item list of the record. The string tag and the full-text pattern search are cross-checks, not second readings, and no agreement statistic exists for either fact.
- Eight eligible works state no first-cell distance in wall units. Four of them hold a member contrast (P02, P31, P34, N707), and twelve member or upper-bound contrasts in seven works carry class N. These cannot be placed on either side of the sublayer and are counted as ordinary members.
- For 159 of the 615 items the source states neither a Mach number nor the equations solved. They are counted inside the primary scope.
- A wall with any prescribed roughness height is recorded rough, as the rule is written, including a pipe roughness of 0.005 mm (N707). Counting that work as smooth would give 105 eligible works and 55 matched-contrast works within the primary scope.
- The description of why the other 50 eligible works hold no matched contrast was not tested for agreement.
- The journal tables of Krank and Wall (2016) and Zhou and Bae (2024) were not accessible. Their values come from the preprints named in the reference list; the first was also checked against the first author's dissertation.

The present scientific claims stand on their stated evidence and limitations. Reproduction checks do not constitute independent scientific replication or journal review.
