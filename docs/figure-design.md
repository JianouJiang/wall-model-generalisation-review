# Figures in the current review

Figure 1 sets the style for all four figures. At the common 160 mm width, each panel starts with a 10 pt bold DejaVu Sans letter and an 8 pt regular heading on the same baseline. Blue (#2a78d6) accents selected marks. Near-black text, warm grey rules and pale grey shading retain their roles throughout. The exported PDFs are checked for font, spacing and overlap.

Figure 1 retains the physical loop, reversed-layer illustration and distinction between prescribed-input and coupled tests. Its profiles are explicitly schematic.

Figure 2 pairs eight selected comparisons across four flow geometries. The channel panel gives the sampling height relative to training and the signed stress errors on three meshes. The flat-plate panel shows how sweep affects predicted friction reversal and how the outer closure defines the error measured in a separate boundary-layer study. On the speed bump the two experiments share a Reynolds number but diagnose reversal from different quantities. In the expansion flows the step panel locates reattachment and the diffuser panel shows the mesh used with each treatment. Each example names its source and the caption identifies the version markers.

The step distances come from the downstream zero crossings of the grey log-law trace, magenta four-step graph-model trace and solid black wall-resolved reference in Dupuy et al., Fig. 16. The figure reports them to the nearest 0.1 step heights. The input data record the plot calibration and two bracketing centreline positions for each trace so that the interpolation can be repeated. These positions are measured independently of the integrated errors in Figure 3. [Dupuy et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1017/dce.2023.2)

Figure 3 pairs the five graph depths from Dupuy et al., Table 5, on aligned scales for wall-stress magnitude and the streamwise component. Each error integral is divided by the law-of-the-wall value for the same quantity. The highlighted four-step row matches the manuscript example. [Dupuy et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1017/dce.2023.2)

The boundary-layer plots share one logarithmic error scale and the four streamwise stations specified in the source's Fig. 9(a). Each plot compares both wall laws under one outer closure. The symbols are approximate local readings from Fig. 9(c,d) and the straight segments join the selected positions. Shading on the two practical-closure plots identifies the authors' 5–15% summary in §4.3. At a streamwise Reynolds number of one million the dynamic Smagorinsky and equilibrium combination gives about 19%. The source prose and the station readings have separate entries in the figure data. [Lozano-Durán and Bae (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2023.331)

Figure 4 has three panels. Panel a is a set of stacked bars, one for all 245 pairs of treatments in the matched contrasts and one for each kind of difference between the two treatments. Each bar shows the composition of its own pairs, so the bars have the same length and the count beside each gives its size. Blue marks a pair whose order changes, grey a pair with one order and white a pair in which nothing is resolved. Panel b gives the same three parts for the 136 contrasts and, on the same scale, the number of contrasts in which the order changes within a profile, between conditions and between quantities. These three are not exclusive, and the last is counted over the contrasts that resolve two classes of quantity under one condition. Panel c shows the 13 retrospective cases as columns and the three recorded choices as rows. A filled circle is a match between the two assessments. Columns are grouped by shared source papers, a bracket joins the cases of a group, and a blue mark identifies the four cases that informed the final step of the checklist. The pilot takes the smallest panel because it establishes feasibility only. Every number is read from results/revision/contrast_outcomes_summary.json or data/checklist_cases.json, and results/figures/fig4_values.json records what was drawn.

The detailed Agrawal correction remains in the research audit. It is one comparison in the review's broader synthesis, not the subject of a dedicated figure.
